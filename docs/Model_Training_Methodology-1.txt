================================================================
MODEL TRAINING METHODOLOGY
Generator + Verifier Architecture for SME IPO Offer Document AI
================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHY TWO MODELS, NOT ONE
--------------------------------------------------------------
A single model that both drafts and checks its own work inherits
its own blind spots — it cannot reliably catch errors it was
confident enough to generate. So the AI layer itself (Stage 2 in
your architecture) is split into two internally distinct models
with different objectives, different training data, and different
failure modes, so they don't fail in the same way at the same time:

  MODEL 1 — GENERATOR ("The Drafter")
    Task: turn structured promoter inputs into SEBI-standard
    disclosure prose and structured tables.

  MODEL 2 — VERIFIER / INSPECTOR ("The Auditor")
    Task: given a drafted section + source data, independently
    judge whether it is compliant, consistent, and traceable —
    NOT by re-generating it, but by scoring and flagging it.

This mirrors how real DRHP review works: one team drafts, a
separate team (legal/compliance) checks — you're just building
that separation into the model layer before it even reaches your
human Stage 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------
1. DATA SOURCES
--------------------------------------------------------------
Data sources are split into CORE (the two datasets each model is
actually trained/fine-tuned on) and SUPPORTING (used for
retrieval grounding, cross-validation, or supplementary signal).
This split matters for the pitch: judges should see that each
model has one clean, defensible, directly-sourced training
corpus — not a vague blend of "various data."

 1.1 CORE DATASET — GENERATOR
     Real RHP / DRHP filings, sourced directly from:
       - SEBI's public filings database (DRHPs and final RHPs
         for mainboard issuers)
       - NSE Emerge (SME platform filing archive)
       - BSE SME (SME platform filing archive)
     Why this is the right core dataset:
       - These are the actual regulator-accepted, final-form
         documents — i.e., the real target output distribution
         the Generator needs to learn to produce
       - SME-platform filings specifically (NSE Emerge / BSE SME)
         are the closest possible match to your target use case,
         since your platform is built for SME issuers, not
         mainboard ones — training primarily on SME-scale RHPs
         means the model learns the right length, complexity, and
         disclosure depth expected for this issuer category
       - Chapter-aligned extraction (Business Overview, Risk
         Factors, Objects of the Issue, Basis for Issue Price,
         Financial Statements) gives clean (structured input →
         approved narrative output) training pairs per section,
         as described in Section 3

 1.2 CORE DATASET — VERIFIER
     SEBI Observation Letters, issued against DRHPs before final
     RHP approval.
     Why this is the right core dataset:
       - Each observation letter is effectively regulator-labeled
         negative-example data: it states, in the regulator's own
         words, exactly what was insufficient, missing, unclear,
         or inconsistent in a given DRHP
       - This is the closest thing to ground-truth "what counts as
         a compliance gap" data available anywhere publicly — no
         other source gives you real regulator judgment at this
         granularity
       - Pairing each observation letter with its corresponding
         DRHP gives (flawed draft, specific deficiency, clause
         reference) triples — exactly the training format the
         Verifier's classification task needs (Section 4.2)

 1.3 SUPPORTING — Regulatory Text Corpus (for RAG, not fine-tuning
     — used by both models at inference time, not as training data)
     - SEBI ICDR Regulations (Issue of Capital and Disclosure
       Requirements)
     - SEBI SME/ICDR framework and applicable circulars/master
       circulars
     - This is kept as a retrieval knowledge base, not baked into
       model weights, so it can be updated the moment SEBI issues
       an amendment without retraining

 1.4 SUPPORTING — Structured/Financial Data
     - Restated financial statements, extracted from the same
       core RHP corpus (1.1) — not a separate source, just a
       different extraction pass over it, used to teach numeric
       consistency patterns
     - Public company financial data APIs (for peer-comparison
       training/validation, e.g., listed SME peer multiples)

 1.5 SUPPORTING — Synthetic Negative Examples (augments the
     Verifier's core dataset, does not replace it)
     - Real observation letters (1.2) are the ground truth, but
       are finite in number — to get enough training volume,
       take clean, approved RHP text from the core Generator
       corpus (1.1) and deliberately corrupt it (swap numbers
       between chapters, remove a mandatory clause, insert an
       unsubstantiated buzzword) to generate additional labeled
       "bad" examples at scale
     - Framed correctly in the pitch as: "real observation letters
       define what deficiency categories look like; synthetic
       corruption gives us volume within those same categories" —
       not a separate, invented data source

--------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA CLEANING & PREPROCESSING PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------
 Step 1 — Document Ingestion
   - OCR (for scanned/older filings) + native PDF/text extraction
   - Strip headers, footers, page numbers, watermarks

 Step 2 — Structural Segmentation
   - Split each DRHP into its canonical chapters (Business
     Overview, Risk Factors, Financial Statements, Objects of the
     Issue, Basis for Issue Price, Promoter/Promoter Group, etc.)
   - Use heading detection (regex + layout-based) since DRHPs
     follow a fairly consistent SEBI-mandated structure

 Step 3 — Entity Tagging (shared with your Rules Engine)
   - Named Entity Recognition to tag financial figures, dates,
     entity names, percentages
   - This produces the same variable_x tagging format used in
     your Cross-Sectional Data Matching Engine, so training data
     and production data share one schema

 Step 4 — Deduplication & Noise Removal
   - Remove boilerplate legal disclaimers that are near-identical
     across filings (low training signal, risk of overfitting to
     fixed phrases) — keep them as templates, not training targets
   - Remove duplicate or near-duplicate filings from the same
     issuer across amendments (keep only the final approved
     version, unless explicitly building a "revision history" task)

 Step 5 — Alignment / Pairing
   - For the Generator: pair structured input fields (financials,
     business description tags) with the corresponding approved
     narrative output — this becomes your input→output training
     pair
   - For the Verifier: pair a document section with (a) a
     compliance label derived from whether it matches the
     SEBI-required schema, and (b) any linked observation-letter
     deficiency, and (c) synthetically corrupted negative variants

 Step 6 — Anonymization Layer (for hackathon-safe handling)
   - Since RHPs and observation letters are both public documents,
     this step is mainly about hygiene, not privacy law — but for
     a demo, mask real company names in a training subset so the
     model doesn't overfit to memorizing specific company facts,
     and instead generalizes the disclosure pattern learned from
     the core NSE Emerge / BSE SME / SEBI corpus

--------------------------------------------------------------
3. MODEL 1 — GENERATOR ("The Drafter")
--------------------------------------------------------------
 3.1 Base Model
   - Start from a strong pretrained LLM (open-weight, e.g., a
     Llama/Mistral-class model, or an API-based foundation model)
     rather than training from scratch — training from scratch is
     infeasible at hackathon scale and unnecessary

 3.2 Training Technique: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) +
     Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
   - SFT on (structured input → approved narrative output) pairs
     teaches the model SEBI's expected tone, structure, and
     phrasing patterns
   - RAG grounds every generation call in the current SEBI ICDR
     text + the promoter's own uploaded data, so the model isn't
     relying purely on memorized patterns — it retrieves the
     actual applicable clause before drafting each section
   - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA / QLoRA) is the
     realistic hackathon approach — full fine-tuning of a large
     model is unnecessary and resource-heavy; LoRA adapters
     trained on the DRHP corpus are enough to specialize style and
     structure while keeping the base model's general reasoning

 3.3 Constrained / Grounded Generation
   - The Generator is restricted to only using facts present in
     the tagged input variables (variable_revenue_FY26, etc.) —
     implemented via prompt-level grounding + a post-generation
     fact-consistency check (does every number in the output
     trace back to an input variable?)
   - This directly reduces hallucination risk, which is the single
     biggest credibility objection judges will raise

 3.4 Task Decomposition
   - Rather than one monolithic "write the whole DRHP" prompt, the
     Generator is called per-section (Risk Factors, Business
     Overview, Objects of the Issue, etc.) with section-specific
     fine-tuned adapters or prompts — smaller, more controllable
     generation tasks, easier to verify, easier to demo

--------------------------------------------------------------
4. MODEL 2 — VERIFIER / INSPECTOR ("The Auditor")
--------------------------------------------------------------
 4.1 Task Framing
   - NOT a generative task — framed as classification + extraction:
     given a drafted section (+ source data + retrieved regulation
     text), output a structured judgment, not free text

 4.2 Training Technique: Multi-Label Classification + Fine-Tuned
     Cross-Encoder
   - A cross-encoder model (jointly reads [drafted text, source
     data, regulation clause] together) trained to output:
     compliant / non-compliant / needs-human-review, per clause
   - Multi-label classification head trained on the labeled
     dataset from Section 1.5 (real SEBI observation letter
     deficiencies + synthetic corruptions) — each label
     corresponds to a deficiency type (missing disclosure,
     numeric mismatch, unsubstantiated claim, materiality
     under-flagging, etc.)

 4.3 Why It's Separate From the Rules Engine
   - The hardcoded Rules Engine (Stage 1, deterministic) catches
     structural/numeric issues with certainty (schema checks,
     entity mismatches) — those don't need ML at all, and
     shouldn't, because they must be 100% reliable
   - The Verifier Model handles the fuzzier judgment calls the
     Rules Engine can't hardcode: "is this risk factor phrased
     with SEBI-standard severity," "does this narrative claim
     match the tone of previously approved filings," "is this
     explanation of litigation risk adequately specific" — pattern
     -based judgment, trained on real historic SEBI feedback

 4.4 Confidence & Explainability Output
   - Every Verifier judgment includes:
     - a confidence score
     - the specific regulation clause it checked against (via the
       same RAG index used by the Generator, so both models share
       one source of truth)
     - a short extracted justification, e.g., "flagged: revenue
       concentration risk described but does not meet SEBI's
       standard specificity threshold seen in comparable approved
       filings"
   - This feeds directly into your Interface B Compliance
     Dashboard and Confidence Scoring (already in your
     architecture spec)

--------------------------------------------------------------
5. HOW THE TWO MODELS INTERACT (Inference-Time Loop)
--------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Structured input (tagged variables) → Generator drafts section
 2. Draft → Rules Engine (deterministic) does structural/numeric
    pass first (cheap, fast, catches hard errors immediately)
 3. Draft that passes Rules Engine → Verifier Model does the
    fuzzier compliance/quality judgment pass
 4. If Verifier flags an issue below a confidence threshold →
    routed back to Generator with the specific flag as additional
    context for a re-draft attempt (self-correction loop, capped
    at N retries to avoid infinite loops)
 5. If Verifier flags an issue it can't resolve with high
    confidence, OR after retry cap is reached → escalated directly
    to the human Intermediary Workbench (Stage 3), never silently
    auto-approved

This retry loop is worth explicitly diagramming in your pitch —
it shows the AI attempting self-correction before ever bothering
a human, which is a strong "efficiency" selling point.

--------------------------------------------------------------
6. OUTPUT FORMAT
--------------------------------------------------------------
Every pipeline run outputs a structured JSON object per section,
not just prose — this is what makes the two interfaces and audit
trail possible:

{
  "section": "Risk Factors",
  "drafted_text": "...",
  "source_variables_used": ["variable_revenue_FY26", "variable_customer_concentration"],
  "rules_engine_status": "pass",
  "verifier_status": "needs_human_review",
  "verifier_confidence": 0.62,
  "verifier_flags": [
    {
      "type": "materiality_specificity",
      "clause_reference": "SEBI ICDR Schedule VI, Risk Factors",
      "justification": "Concentration risk stated without quantified percentage"
    }
  ],
  "retry_count": 1,
  "last_modified_by": "generator_model_v1",
  "audit_trail": [ ... ]
}

This structured output is what gets rendered differently in each
interface (plain summary for the promoter, full detail for the
intermediary) and is what gets exported as the final audit trail
once a human certifies and locks the document.

--------------------------------------------------------------
7. EVALUATION / DEMO METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------
For the hackathon demo, define measurable metrics judges can see:
 - Generator: % of drafted facts traceable to source input
   variables (fact-consistency rate)
 - Verifier: precision/recall against your held-out labeled set
   of real SEBI observation-letter deficiencies + synthetic errors
 - End-to-end: time-to-first-complete-draft on your one concrete
   demo scenario (e.g., a textile manufacturer SME IPO), and
   number of issues caught pre-submission vs. what would typically
   surface as a SEBI clarification query post-submission

--------------------------------------------------------------
8. HACKATHON-SCALE FEASIBILITY NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------
You will not train these from scratch or fine-tune a huge model
in the time available — and you don't need to claim you will. For
the idea round, the credible framing is:
 - Generator: RAG + prompting + LoRA fine-tuning on a modest
   curated set of real RHP sections pulled from SEBI, NSE Emerge,
   and BSE SME filing archives (dozens to low hundreds of
   examples is enough to demonstrate the concept)
 - Verifier: a smaller classifier trained on the labeled
   deficiency dataset built from SEBI observation letters (core)
   + synthetic corruption of the same RHP corpus (volume booster)
   — feasible to build a working proof-of-concept for one or two
   chapters (e.g., Risk Factors + Objects of the Issue) within a
   hackathon build window
 - Explicitly state in your pitch that full-corpus training and
   coverage of all DRHP chapters is the production roadmap beyond
   the hackathon — judges respect a team that's clear about what's
   prototyped vs. what's the long-term vision, rather than
   overclaiming a fully trained production system already exists
