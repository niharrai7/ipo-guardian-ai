================================================================
STANDOUT FEATURES / KEY DIFFERENTIATORS
(Judge-facing summary — "the problem is real, and this solves it")
================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------
1. THREE-STAGE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - AI drafts → Hardcoded Rules validate → Human intermediary
   certifies
 - No stage has unilateral authority — the AI can never submit
   anything, the rules can never be overridden silently, and the
   human is never asked to review a blind, unstructured wall of
   AI text
 - Directly answers the core regulatory objection every judge
   will have: "can we trust AI-drafted disclosure documents?" —
   answer: you're not trusting the AI alone, you're trusting a
   system where AI output is gated twice before a human even
   sees it

--------------------------------------------------------------
2. DUAL INTERFACE (Company + Intermediary)
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - Interface A (Company/Promoter): guided, plain-language,
   accessible to a first-time SME promoter with zero capital-
   markets background
 - Interface B (Merchant Banker / Legal / Auditor): professional
   review workbench — clause-level compliance view, redlining,
   flag resolution, role-based certification
 - Mirrors the real-world division of responsibility in an actual
   IPO filing instead of collapsing everyone into one generic
   chat UI
 - Preserves the exact thing SEBI's own problem statement asks
   for: intermediaries keep their review and certification role

--------------------------------------------------------------
3. TWO-STEP AI MODEL (Generator + Verifier) FOR ACCURACY
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - Not one model doing everything — a Generator model that drafts,
   and a separate Verifier model that independently judges the
   draft, so the system doesn't inherit a single model's blind
   spots
 - Generator trained on real, regulator-accepted RHPs from SEBI,
   NSE Emerge, and BSE SME filing archives
 - Verifier trained on SEBI Observation Letters — real,
   regulator-authored records of exactly what was found
   deficient in past filings
 - This is the single most defensible data claim in the pitch:
   both models are trained on real regulatory outcomes, not
   generic internet text or assumptions about what "good"
   disclosure looks like

--------------------------------------------------------------
4. HARDCODED RULES ENGINE (Non-Negotiable Layer)
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - Deterministic, not probabilistic — critical checks are never
   left to AI judgment
 - Runs both before AI drafting (input validation) and after
   (output validation), so nothing reaches a human unchecked
 - This is what makes the platform SEBI-credible rather than
   "just another AI chatbot" — hard rules for hard requirements,
   AI only for language generation

--------------------------------------------------------------
5. UI/UX, SECURITY & AUTHENTICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - Purpose-built, role-based UI/UX for non-expert company users —
   not a generic form, a guided disclosure journey
 - Strong security and authentication layer for a document
   handling confidential financials, promoter family data, and
   pre-IPO material information (details to be finalized —
   role-based access control, encryption at rest/in transit,
   audit-logged access)

--------------------------------------------------------------
6. AI-DETECTED ISSUE TYPES (What the Model Actually Catches)
--------------------------------------------------------------
 6.1 Format & Structural Gaps
     - Missing mandatory sections or sub-sections per SEBI's
       required disclosure schema

 6.2 Entity Extraction + Cross-Check Graph
     - Every critical figure (revenue, PAT, litigation value,
       promoter shareholding) tagged as a variable and tracked
       across every chapter it appears in
     - Flags mismatches between chapters automatically (e.g., a
       number that doesn't match itself across the document)

 6.3 Compliance Classification
     - Classifies whether each disclosed section meets SEBI's
       mandatory disclosure requirements, section by section

 6.4 Buzzword / Unsubstantiated Claim Filter
     - Detects subjective, unquantified claims ("market leader,"
       "state-of-the-art," "rapidly growing") — the same category
       of check SEBI's own review process looks for — and flags or
       strips them unless backed by a verifiable source

 6.5 Official Government Source Verification
     - MCA21 checks: director/company status, DIN/CIN verification
     - Background / disqualification checks: struck-off,
       defunct, or insolvency-linked entities flagged automatically
     - Built on India's Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)
       framework (API Setu and equivalent), so verification uses
       the same underlying government data sources SEBI itself
       would reference

 6.6 Automated Financial Data Entry
     - Stage 1 AI reads uploaded financial records (statements,
       reports) and auto-populates the relevant document fields,
       removing manual re-typing and the transcription errors that
       come with it

 6.7 Promoter Group / Family Relation Mapping
     - Maps promoter relatives (spouse, parents, siblings) and
       associated entities to catch undisclosed related-party
       connections before they become a SEBI rejection reason

 6.8 Document Cleanup & Organization
     - Takes messy, unstructured promoter input and organizes it
       into the correctly sequenced, properly formatted disclosure
       document structure

--------------------------------------------------------------
7. SPECIAL EMPHASIS: OBJECTS OF THE ISSUE + RISK FACTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------
 - These two sections are treated as the highest-priority,
   highest-scrutiny part of the entire platform
 - Mandatorily routed through all three stages (AI draft → rules
   validation → human certification) with no shortcut path,
   regardless of how "complete" the AI's confidence score is
 - The Generator and Verifier models are specifically trained
   with extra weight/emphasis on these two sections, because:
     - Risk Factors: a significant share of real SEBI observation
       letters relate to inadequate or vague risk factor
       disclosure — this is where regulator scrutiny concentrates
       most heavily in practice
     - Objects of the Issue: requires exact fund-allocation
       justification, vendor verification, and utilization
       clarity — one of the most commonly under-specified sections
       in SME filings
 - Practical implication for the pitch: "we didn't build one
   generic AI for the whole document — we identified, from real
   observation letter data, exactly where SEBI's scrutiny
   concentrates, and built our most rigorous checks there"
